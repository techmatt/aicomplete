## Presence destruction, measured (engine, d2, 32 seeds)

War 38%, Discord cards 33%, tier-2 threats 16%, corruption 13%. No source above about half.
**Tier-1 threats 0%** at d2 for the engine: Eviction's choice is always paid, which is the
choice working. The brief expected uncountered threats as the main source; countered threats
don't get there. Over all agents in the screen: Discord 33, war 23, tier-2 20, corruption 18,
tier-1 6 (random and greedy let threats through).

**The presence loss is real for a team that ignores presence, not yet for one that plays.**
0 of 64 engine games. `turtle` and `no_presence` lose by presence in 126 of 128 games, mostly at
rounds 4-6. A team that builds holds 5-6 tokens, a hit takes one hex, and a token costs 3 credits to
put back. Letting troops strike presence from stage III (`troop_presence` 3) gave 4 presence
losses in 32 games, but took 10-19 presence a game and 70% of it from the march, which fails
the ledger, so it stays off. Raising `presence_credits` to 6 made no presence losses and cut
wins. If a presence loss should reach ordinary bad games, that's the design question to take
up: see *Presence*, below.

**The structural cause.** Science, Industry, Cyber and Power still don't earn their keep, as in
024, though no longer because the game ends at a printed round: wins now run to rounds 8-10.
What decides a game is a push (ambassadors, Diplomacy) and counters (credits, Security). Those

one-action-at-a-time agents don't plan for that; a deeper agent might. That no-powers is level
says AI-board powers aren't pulling weight either. I did not tune around this.

## Loops, and how each is capped

- **Arrivals → corruption → Discord → harsher cards and extra threats → presence and corruption.**
  Capped by the Discord deck ending the game: at most 6 cards ever resolve. It starts late: first
  corruption is rare before stage II, and the stage-I last-presence rule closes the early hole.
  The earliest engine loss at d2 is round 7. Cutting `city_build` and `node_compute` to 1 is what
  stopped the first version running away by round 6.
- **Freeing → Unity → an easier purge → freeing matters more.** Capped by the hold, which costs
  2-5 ambassadors at 6 credits each. **Ambassadors → frees → Unity cards → more ambassadors**
  doesn't close: no Unity card gives ambassadors, income cards buy one or two, and only 9 Unity
  cards exist. Frees run at 2-3 a game.
- **Countering costs credits, lost presence loses income.** No spiral at d2: winners lose 2
  presence a game and buy them back. Teams that stop countering die of Discord (`ignore_threats`
  loses at rounds 5-8), not of the spiral.

## The end conditions, for the rules review

**Purge.** The real win: every engine win. Wins spread over rounds 4-10 with no gate, and the
fight lasts: frees are 2-3 a game, a freed city keeps an ambassador, and arrivals keep coming. The
ladder is the pacing: 2 tips ease ambassadors and troops, 4 ease nodes. Nodes peak at 5-8 on the
board. One win in 64 came at round 4; I'd leave it, a gate is what this item removed. The hold is 2 + adversary ambassadors rather than the
brief's 3, because agents that act one step at a time can't gather 5-6 ambassadors. A stronger
player might manage 3, which is a reason to retest with a deeper agent.

**The last Unity card.** It has not happened in an engine game: 4 tips of 9. It is a cap that
stops the Unity deck being spent forever more than a way to win. If it should win games, cut the
deck to 6-7.

**Discord deck.** The main loss, and it plays as it should: a clock you can count, ending
around rounds 8-10. The escalation had to be redone: with no
reshuffle the draw order is fixed, so steps moved to tips that can be reached. The stage-III sway
keeps a losing 2-country team from sliding to the deck-out.
**Presence.** A sharp punishment for not building it, and otherwise rarely close. Winners lose 2,
losers 4, and nobody who places presence gets near 0. The stage-I rule did little for the engine
and exists for the opening. It works as a floor but not as a live threat. To make it live, the
hit has to outrun a 3-credit token: troops striking presence do that but flood the ledger. A
"destroy all presence in the country" late card, or a higher cost to replace destroyed tokens
only, is worth trying; both are bigger than this item.

**Deck-out.** Back to the outer clock: 5 of 64 at d2. Worth keeping as it is.

**Supply.** A cap now, and it almost never bites below d3.

## How the game ends, for a new player

You win by purging the board or by drawing the last Unity card. Pressure comes every round: the
face-up adversary card names a kind of site, and every such site gains an adversary ambassador, so
you always know where the next wave lands. Ambassadors pile up, pairs become nodes, six on a city
corrupt it, and corrupted cities and uncountered threats push the Alignment track toward Discord.
Each time the track tips, a Discord card hurts you, and the Discord deck is small: when a tip
finds it empty, you lose. Pushing back works the same way in your favour. Mass your ambassadors on
a corrupted city to free it, which sways toward Unity, and each Unity tip brings a good card. Two
Unity tips and the purge asks only for no nodes and no corrupted cities; four and it asks only for
no corrupted cities. You also lose if any player has no presence left on the board (threats,
Discord cards, war and corruption destroy it; in stage I your last one is safe), or if the
adversary deck runs out. So every game is a race between two small decks.

**What resisted a summary:** "next to" in the hold (each adversary ambassador on or next to the
city adds 1, so the number moves as you clear) takes a worked example at the table. "Most
threatened hex" needs a definition on the card. The difference between the last-presence rule
(stage I, everything) and Eviction (always, only that threat) doesn't fit a sentence.

## What changed for the agent

`eval.rs` now values gathering ambassadors toward a city's hold and a climbing bonus for landing
them, values a seat's last presence, and watches the Discord deck instead of the supply. At d4 the
agents' guessed Discord deck keeps the true contents, so it knows which card was removed; a small
leak, left in.
