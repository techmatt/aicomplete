# Rulebook friction (rules revision 73)

*What was hard to say in [rulebook.md](rulebook.md), ranked by how much each costs a reader. Matt's test: a rule that
can't be said well is a bad sign about the rule. Written by 172 from the engine. Every rule here was read in the code;
the per-area working papers (each engine fact with its file:line) are in
[experiments/results/172_rulebook/](../experiments/results/172_rulebook/).*

**How it's ranked.** Cost is how often a player meets the rule (every round, most games, rarely) times how much it asks
of them: an exception ("unless", "except", "but if"), a forward reference or a term used once, more than about three
sentences to state precisely, or a result a player wouldn't predict from the board or card. A rule met every round that
needs an exception ranks above a rare rule that needs five sentences.

**Length.** The core rules (sections 1-17, without the Sources lines, the legend or the glossary) run about **8,000
words** (counted with a word regex over the Markdown), the whole page about 9,200. Spirit Island's base-game core rules
(setup through the end of the game, without its spirit, adversary, scenario and glossary pages) are about 12-14
rulebook pages at roughly 500 words of rules text a page, so about **6,000-8,000 words**: an estimate from memory of
the page count, not a count (a library record gives the whole rulebook as 31 pages). In words this draft is at the top
of that range. But it has **no diagrams and no worked examples**, which
take about a third of Spirit Island's pages, and it leaves out all card text; a finished rulebook would run roughly
**1.4-1.7× Spirit Island's core**. Its density is the sharper signal: 47 entries below, 21 of them in rules met every
round.

## The five worst

1. **Corruption** (#1): the adversary's core verb is a counting procedure with a cascade, done by hand every round.
2. **Ambassadors meeting agents** (#2): three exchange rates for one contact, and the action named after it is the
   exception.
3. **Claim against Liberate** (#3): sibling actions that differ in four ways, none visible on the board.
4. **The destruction ladder** (#4): six protections in a fixed order, with war's node loss bypassing all but one.
5. **Two kinds of troop** (#5): the same word for two pieces that move, target and are made differently.
